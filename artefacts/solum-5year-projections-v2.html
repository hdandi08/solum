How This Model Differs From v1
v1 modelled continuous acquisition from month 1. This model reflects how SOLUM actually launches: Phase 1 (Months 1–2) is a limited one-time run — 250 units sold to early adopters, 50 gifted for promo, zero subscription infrastructure. Phase 2 (Month 3+) is when subscriptions launch, first-batch buyers convert to founding subscribers, and paid acquisition begins in earnest.

The model derives all numbers from locked unit economics: avg first box £71 (70% GROUND / 30% RITUAL mix), avg sub £41/mo, avg sub GP £21.90/mo (after COGS). Postage £3.85 deducted from first-box GP. The three scenarios differ only in new customers/month, CAC, and churn rate. Everything else flows from those three inputs.

The Levers That Drive Everything
Lever 01 — The Most Important
Churn Rate
Going from 7.5% to 5.5% churn (conservative → optimistic) is the difference between £582K and £2.33M in 5-year net profit — nearly 4× on the same product. Churn is driven by product quality, ritual depth, and how well packaging communicates the routine. Every creative decision either reduces churn or it does not.
Lever 02 — Phase 1 Conversion
Founding Subs
Converting 60% vs 30% of your first 250 buyers (150 vs 75 founding subscribers) gives you double the founding MRR at Month 3 before a single paid ad runs. This conversion hinges entirely on Phase 1 experience quality — product, packaging, ritual card, follow-up email. It is free money if you get it right.
Lever 03 — Paid Growth Engine
Ad Spend
Because CAC payback is Month 1 across all scenarios, every pound of ad spend is recovered immediately. Conservative runs on £1,280/mo and still reaches £280K/year by Year 5. The difference between conservative and optimistic at the ad level is just £1,840/mo extra. The model is self-funding from the moment subscriptions launch.
Lever 04 — Phase 1 Gifting
50 Free Kits
The 50 gifted units cost roughly £895 in product COGS and generate zero direct revenue. But if gifting to athletes, creators, and micro-influencers drives even 10% organic acquisition uplift, it reduces effective CAC significantly. This is the highest-leverage spend in the model — treat it as a media buy, not a cost.
